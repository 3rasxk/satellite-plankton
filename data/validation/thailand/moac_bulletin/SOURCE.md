# MOAC (Ministry of Agriculture and Cooperatives) disaster bulletin — extracted event

**Not a dataset** — a single event extracted from one dated narrative disaster-monitoring bulletin found while researching Thailand's Department of Fisheries (which itself does not appear to host a red-tide/HAB dataset — red tide monitoring is DMCR's mandate, not DOF's; DOF's relevant contribution is aquaculture damage reports like this one, used as an indirect bloom proxy).

**Source:** Ministry of Agriculture and Cooperatives (MOAC), Thailand — disaster/warning bulletin dated 15 June 2561 (2018 CE). https://www.moac.go.th/warning-files-401391791087
**Date accessed:** 2026-08-18
**Record count: 1 row** — a mollusc die-off in Samut Songkhram explicitly attributed to a plankton bloom causing oxygen depletion. The same bulletin also describes two other unrelated agricultural incidents (a fish die-off in Songkhla attributed to stagnant water/low oxygen from dense farming, and a pesticide-related fish kill in Phitsanulok) — these were NOT included since they aren't attributed to an algal bloom.
**License:** Thai government bulletin; no explicit license found — treat as citable public information, not a licensed open dataset.
**Citation:** see above.

## Why this matters despite being a single row
This is the pattern the task explicitly flagged as worth checking: aquaculture/fisheries damage reports that log bloom events indirectly through fish-kill or shellfish-closure records, even when no dedicated HAB database exists. **No structured/tabular archive of these MOAC bulletins was found** — this was a single bulletin surfaced by a research agent, not a systematic search of MOAC's full bulletin history. If more Thai bloom-proxy events are needed, systematically working through MOAC's and DOF's dated disaster-bulletin archives (if one exists in a browsable/listable form) is a plausible next step, but was not attempted here given the scope of this pass.
